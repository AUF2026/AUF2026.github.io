 ## 1\. Purpose

 This document defines the claim-level registry used to trace each AUF2026 mathematical result from its source to its formalization, validation, prior-art comparison and documented assessment.

 The registry separates:

- author identity;
- source corpus;
- mathematical claims;
- exact statements;
- definitions;
- assumptions;
- dependencies;
- derivations;
- formal artifacts;
- computational artifacts;
- prior literature;
- mathematical comparisons;
- applications;
- evidence status.

 The registry does not treat the AUF2026 corpus as one undifferentiated mathematical claim.

 The fundamental traceability chain is:

 ↓\

 ## 3\. Evidence levels

 Each claim receives an evidence level according to the evidence actually available.

 ### E0 — Not examined

 No claim-level examination has yet been performed.

 ### E1 — Corpus documented

 The relevant AUF2026 artifact has been located and registered.

 ### E2 — Statement extracted

 The exact mathematical statement has been extracted from the authoritative source.

 ### E3 — Formal artifact identified

 A corresponding Lean, symbolic or computational formal artifact has been identified.

 ### E4 — Formal verification documented

 The formal artifact has been compiled or otherwise verified in the declared environment and the supporting validation evidence is preserved.

 ### E5 — Prior-art comparison completed

 Relevant mathematical literature has been examined at claim level.

 ### E6 — External assessment documented

 A qualified external mathematical assessment is available.

 These levels should not be inferred merely from chronology.

 A claim may move directly from E2 to E5, for example, if prior-art examination occurs before formalization.

 The registry records evidence that actually exists.

---

 ## 4\. Originality dimensions

 Originality is not represented by one numerical value.

 The following dimensions are recorded separately:

 1. Statement originality
2. Definition originality
3. Construction originality
4. Derivation originality
5. Proof-method originality
6. Generality
7. Formal or computational contribution
8. Application contribution
9. Relationship to prior literature

 These dimensions must not be collapsed into a single conclusion.

 A formally verified theorem may correspond to an established mathematical result.

 A new construction may use established mathematical machinery.

 A new proof may concern an established theorem.

 A new formalization may formalize mathematics already known.

 Therefore:

 FORMAL VERIFICATION ≠ MATHEMATICAL NOVELTY

 Likewise:

 BIBLIOGRAPHIC SIMILARITY ≠ MATHEMATICAL IDENTITY

 ## 19\. Prior-literature comparison protocol

 Prior-art analysis proceeds through multiple search layers.

 The purpose is to reduce false negatives caused by:

 - terminology changes;
- notation changes;
- different mathematical languages;
- different disciplinary conventions;
- alternative formulations.

 ### Layer 1 — Exact terminology

 Search:

 - exact theorem terminology;
- distinctive mathematical expressions;
- named constructions;
- characteristic phrases.

 This layer is for discovery.

 A terminology match is not by itself evidence of mathematical identity.

 ### Layer 2 — Definitions

 Search the definitions underlying the claim independently of AUF2026 terminology.

 Include:

 - introduced definitions;
- specialized notation;
- structural conditions;
- invariant definitions;
- mappings;
- algebraic objects;
- geometric objects.

 ### Layer 3 — Mathematical objects

 Search the mathematical objects independently of the terminology used by the corpus.

 Examples include:

 - matrices;
- operators;
- transformations;
- tuples;
- groups;
- semigroups;
- fixed-point structures;
- dynamical systems;
- geometric structures;
- algebraic structures;
- computational constructions.

 ### Layer 4 — Equivalent formulations

 Search alternative formulations using:

 - different notation;
- different terminology;
- equivalent definitions;
- alternative representations;
- equivalent theorem statements.

 ### Layer 5 — Classical antecedents

 Search foundational literature for:

 - underlying theorems;
- lemmas;
- constructions;
- mathematical machinery;
- historical formulations.

 ### Layer 6 — Modern literature

 Search contemporary research literature for:

 - equivalent results;
- stronger variants;
- weaker variants;
- generalizations;
- special cases;
- independent constructions;
- alternative formulations;
- applications;
- computational implementations.

 ### Layer 7 — Formal mathematics

 Where relevant, examine:

 - Lean;
- Mathlib;
- other proof assistants;
- formal theorem repositories;
- machine-verified mathematical corpora.

 The presence of an underlying theorem in a formal library does not automatically establish that the complete AUF2026 construction is represented there.

 ### Layer 8 — Cross-domain terminology

 When a construction may occur in multiple mathematical fields, search terminology from each relevant field.

 The search must not be restricted to terminology introduced by AUF2026.

 ## 20\. Comparison rule

 A bibliographic match is not established from:

 - title similarity;
- keyword similarity;
- author surname;
- abstract similarity alone;
- superficial formula similarity;
- shared terminology;
- citation proximity.

 A relevant comparison requires examination of the mathematical proposition itself.

 The comparison must answer two distinct questions:

 **What exactly was already known?**

 **What exactly does the AUF2026 claim contain?**

 The second question must not be answered before the first has been documented.

 If the available evidence is insufficient, the status remains unresolved.

 ## 22\. Statement-level comparison

 The first comparison must be between mathematical statements.

 For every candidate prior result determine:

 1. whether the domains coincide;
2. whether the mathematical objects coincide;
3. whether the definitions coincide;
4. whether the hypotheses coincide;
5. whether the conclusions coincide;
6. whether one statement implies the other;
7. whether the statements are equivalent;
8. whether they overlap only partially;
9. whether they concern different objects despite similar terminology.

 Whenever possible, express the comparison mathematically.

 Example:

 Prior result:

 H₀ ⟹ C₀

 AUF2026 result:

 H₁ ⟹ C₁

 Then compare:

 H₀ versus H₁

 C₀ versus C₁

 domain₀ versus domain₁

 object₀ versus object₁

 Different notation alone must not be treated as a mathematical difference.

---

 ## 23\. Construction-level comparison

 When a theorem depends on a construction, the construction must itself be compared.

 Record:

 - input objects;
- transformation rules;
- intermediate structures;
- invariants;
- output object;
- existence conditions;
- uniqueness conditions;
- dimensional assumptions;
- algebraic assumptions;
- topological assumptions;
- computational implementation where relevant.

 Two results may have related conclusions but materially different constructions.

 Conversely, apparently different constructions may be mathematically equivalent.

 Such equivalence must be established from mathematical analysis rather than terminology.

---

 ## 24\. Proof-level comparison

 Proof originality is a separate dimension.

 Where information is available, record:

 - principal proof strategy;
- central lemma;
- transformations;
- invariants;
- fixed-point arguments;
- algebraic arguments;
- geometric arguments;
- analytic arguments;
- computational arguments;
- formal proof architecture.

 The following distinctions remain explicit:

 NEW THEOREM ≠ NEW PROOF

 NEW PROOF ≠ NEW THEOREM

 NEW FORMALIZATION ≠ NEW PROOF

 NEW PROOF ARCHITECTURE ≠ NEW MATHEMATICAL STATEMENT

 A new proof of an established theorem may constitute a distinct mathematical contribution without changing the theorem statement.

 ## 26\. Bibliographic evidence hierarchy

 Primary mathematical sources should receive priority.

 The preferred order is:

 1. original research article;
2. original book or monograph;

4. formal theorem library;
5. authoritative institutional repository;

7. secondary database;
8. search-engine result used only for discovery.

 Search snippets and bibliographic aggregators are discovery tools.

 They are not substitutes for examining the underlying mathematical source.

 ## 29\. Negative-result protocol

 Failure to locate a prior result must not automatically be written as:

 NO PRIOR RESULT EXISTS

 unless the evidence actually supports such a conclusion.

 The search record should preserve:

 - databases;
- search engines;
- queries;
- dates;
- filters;
- literature examined;
- relevant negative findings.

 A negative search result is always relative to its documented search scope.

 ## 31\. Formal verification relation

 Formal verification is recorded independently from mathematical originality.

 The formal chain is:

 MATHEMATICAL STATEMENT\
 ↓\
 FORMAL STATEMENT\
 ↓\
 FORMAL PROOF\
 ↓\
 PROOF TERM\
 ↓\
 KERNEL CHECKING

 A successful kernel check provides evidence concerning the formalized proposition within the declared formal environment.

 It does not by itself establish:

 - mathematical originality;
- historical priority;
- empirical validity;
- physical interpretation;
- engineering usefulness;
- publication significance.

 Conversely, absence of a formalization does not by itself establish:

 - incorrectness;
- lack of originality;
- lack of mathematical significance.

 Formal verification is therefore one evidence dimension among several.

 ## 36\. Application relationship

 An application is not automatically a theorem consequence.

 For each proposed application distinguish:

 THEOREM\
 ↓\
 DERIVED MATHEMATICAL CONSEQUENCE\
 ↓\
 COMPUTATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION\
 ↓\
 MODEL\
 ↓\
 PHYSICAL OR ENGINEERING APPLICATION

 Each transition requires its own evidence.

 A theorem may establish a mathematical result without establishing every empirical or engineering consequence attributed to an implementation.

 Likewise, empirical agreement does not replace a mathematical proof.

 ## 37\. Generality analysis

 Each theorem record should identify its domain.

 ### Domain

 Where does the theorem apply?

 ### Dimension

 The relevant setting may be:

 - finite;
- countable;
- finite-dimensional;
- infinite-dimensional;
- continuous;
- discrete;
- mixed;
- another explicitly defined mathematical setting.

 ### Assumptions

 Which assumptions are required?

 ### Dependence

 Which hypotheses are required for each conclusion?

 ### Extension

 Does the theorem extend beyond its canonical construction?

 If so, the extension must be stated precisely.

 ### Limitation

 Where does the theorem cease to apply?

 ### Stability

 Where relevant, determine whether the result is stable under:

 - perturbation;
- approximation;
- discretization;
- representation changes.

 ### Boundary cases

 Record degenerate, limiting and exceptional cases.

 ## 38\. Claim classification

 Each theorem claim may eventually receive one or more factual states.

 ### FORMALIZED

 A formal mathematical statement exists.

 ### FORMALLY VERIFIED

 The corresponding formal artifact has been verified in the declared environment.

 ### BIBLIOGRAPHICALLY COMPARED

 Relevant prior literature has been examined.

 ### MATHEMATICALLY DISTINGUISHED

 The comparison identifies concrete mathematical differences from cited prior results.

 ### EXTERNALLY ASSESSED

 An external qualified assessment is documented.

 ### APPLICATION VERIFIED

 A claimed application has independent supporting evidence.

 These states are independent.

 For example:

 FORMALLY VERIFIED\
 +\
 BIBLIOGRAPHICALLY COMPARED\
 +\
 MATHEMATICALLY DISTINGUISHED

 does not imply:

 EXTERNALLY ASSESSED

 unless such an assessment is actually documented.

 ## 39\. No composite novelty score

 The registry deliberately avoids a single numerical originality score.

 The dimensions are structurally different:

 FORMAL CORRECTNESS ≠ NOVELTY

 NOVELTY ≠ GENERALITY

 GENERALITY ≠ IMPACT

 IMPACT ≠ APPLICATION VALIDITY

 A theorem may be:

 - formally verified but not yet compared against prior literature;
- compared against literature but not formally verified;
- mathematically distinguished from identified results but awaiting external assessment;
- supported by computational evidence without that evidence constituting a proof;
- based on established mathematical machinery while containing a distinct construction.

 The registry records these states separately.

 86. Separation of evidence domains

 The registry must keep separate:

 MATHEMATICAL EVIDENCE\
 FORMAL EVIDENCE\
 COMPUTATIONAL EVIDENCE\
 BIBLIOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE\
 EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE\
 EXTERNAL ASSESSMENT

 Evidence from one domain should not automatically substitute for evidence from another.

 For example:

 formal verification does not establish historical priority;

 bibliographic similarity does not establish mathematical equivalence;

 numerical agreement does not establish a formal proof;

 empirical agreement does not establish a mathematical derivation.

---

 87. Claim-level status model

 A theorem record may contain multiple simultaneous states.

 Example:

 FORMALIZED: YES\
 FORMALLY VERIFIED: YES\
 BIBLIOGRAPHICALLY COMPARED: YES\
 MATHEMATICALLY DISTINGUISHED: OPEN\
 EXTERNALLY ASSESSED: NO\
 APPLICATION VERIFIED: OPEN

 This multidimensional representation is preferred to a single overall status.

 The registry should therefore avoid collapsing multiple evidence dimensions into one label.

---

 88. Uncertainty representation

 Uncertainty should be recorded explicitly.

 Recommended values include:

 ESTABLISHED\
 SUPPORTED\
 PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED\
 OPEN\
 UNRESOLVED\
 CONTRADICTED\
 NOT APPLICABLE

 The selected state must be supported by the available evidence.

 Uncertainty should not be hidden through definitive language when the underlying comparison remains incomplete.

---

 89. Corpus expansion beyond T1–T12

 T1–T12 represent only the initial controlled theorem registry.

 Additional claims should be added when corpus ingestion identifies:

 - independent theorems;
- independent lemmas;
- materially distinct constructions;
- formally distinct propositions;
- mathematically substantive corollaries;
- independent derivations.

 The registry should avoid creating duplicate identifiers for different representations of the same mathematical claim.

 91. Final operational statement

 The theorem originality matrix is complete only when each registered claim has a visible evidence state.

 The matrix does not need to resolve every originality question immediately.

 It must instead make unresolved questions explicit and preserve the path required to resolve them.

 The canonical principle remains:

 ONE CLAIM\
 ↓\
 ONE SOURCE\
 ↓\
 ONE STATEMENT\
 ↓\
 ONE EVIDENCE CHAIN

 This is the final operational basis for the AUF2026 theorem-level registry.
